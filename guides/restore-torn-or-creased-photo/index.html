How to restore a torn or creased photo

By Our Decades · Updated November 25, 2026

Don’t tape it. Scan the print with the torn pieces laid together, and repair the scan: a crease or a clean tear can be made nearly invisible on the copy, while the print itself stays safe in an envelope.

First, protect the print

  • No tape and no glue. Ordinary tape yellows, stains the picture, and is very hard to remove later.
  • Hold the print by its edges, with clean, dry hands.
  • Keep every piece, even the small ones, flat in a paper envelope or folder.
  • Don’t force a curled or brittle print flat. It can crack.

Scan it

  1. Lay the pieces on a flat, plain surface.
  2. Fit the torn edges together as closely as you can, without overlapping them.
  3. Use even light from in front, such as daylight from a window. Light from one side throws a shadow along a crease and makes it look worse.
  4. Hold your iPhone level, directly above the print, and take the photo without flash.

What can be repaired on the copy

  • Creases, fold lines, scratches and small cracks: yes. The picture on both sides of the line shows what belongs there.
  • A clean tear where the pieces still meet: yes, and usually it becomes nearly invisible.
  • A missing corner or a missing piece of background, such as sky, a wall or grass: it can be filled in believably, but that is a fill, not the original.
  • A missing or badly damaged face: no. Anything drawn there is invented, and an invented face is a different person.

By hand or automatically

By hand, you paint over the line with a retouching tool in a photo editor. It’s slow, and you control every stroke. A restoration app does it in one step. Either way, compare the result with your scan, and look hardest at the faces.

How Our Decades does it

Creases, scratches, dust, fading and yellowing are fixed. A torn photo is fixed in part: what’s torn away can’t come back as it was. The AI is told not to add anything, and we check that no face is made up. We’d rather do less than turn someone into a stranger.

When to see a professional

Digital restoration gives you a good picture. It doesn’t repair the paper. If the print itself matters, for example an only copy that is cracking or one stuck to the glass of its frame, a photograph conservator can stabilize it.

Questions

Should I tape a torn photo back together?

No. Tape yellows, stains the picture and is very hard to remove. Keep the pieces flat in a paper envelope, and scan them laid together instead.

Can a photo torn in half be restored?

Usually, if you have both halves. Lay them together, scan them as one picture, and the tear line can be repaired on the copy.

Can a missing part of a photo be restored?

Background can be filled in believably. A missing face can’t be restored honestly, because anything drawn there is invented.

Can creases be removed from an old photo?

Yes, on a scan. Creases and fold lines are among the easiest damage to repair, because the picture on both sides of the line is still there.
