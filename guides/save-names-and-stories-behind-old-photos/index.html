How to save the names and stories behind old photos

By Our Decades · Updated November 25, 2026

Ask now, while someone still knows, and keep the answer with the photo itself. A name kept in a separate notebook, or in one person’s memory, gets lost. A name saved with the picture travels with it.

Why now

A photo can last a hundred years. The last person who can say who is in it won’t. Most families find this out one relative too late.

How to ask

  • Take one album at a time, for twenty or thirty minutes
  • Show the photos large, on a tablet or a TV if you can
  • Ask before you record, then record the conversation: the voice is a keepsake too
  • Don’t hurry and don’t correct. “About 1962” is better than nothing
  • Use the days the family is already together

What to ask

  • Who is this, and how are they related to us?
  • Where was this, and about what year?
  • What was happening that day?
  • What do you remember about them?
  • Who took the picture?

Where to keep the answers

  • With the digital photo. On an iPhone, open the photo in Photos, swipe up, and type in “Add a Caption”. The caption stays with the photo.
  • On the print, if you must: a soft pencil, lightly, on the back near the edge. Never a ballpoint or a felt-tip pen, which press through or bleed, and never on the front.
  • On a slip of paper in the sleeve beside the print.

Write full names

Write “Mary Ellen Carter, my mother’s mother”, not “Grandma”. In fifty years nobody will know whose grandma.

How Our Decades does it

Hold the mic and talk: the words appear as you speak. Record the story in their own voice, and add the names and the date. It all stays with the photo.

Questions

How do I label old photos without damaging them?

Use a soft pencil, lightly, on the back near the edge. Never use a ballpoint or felt-tip pen, and never write on the front. Safer still, keep the names with the scanned copy.

What should I ask relatives about old family photos?

Who is in the picture and how they’re related, where it was taken and about what year, what was happening that day, and who took the picture.

How do I identify people in old family photos?

Ask your oldest relatives first, and send copies to cousins in other branches of the family. Look on the back for names and photo studio stamps, and compare faces with photos that are already labeled.

Where should I keep the names and dates?

With the photo itself, as a caption on the digital copy, so they travel together wherever the photo is sent.
