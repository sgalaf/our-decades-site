How to scan a photo album with your iPhone

By Our Decades · Updated November 25, 2026

Lay the album flat near a window, hold your iPhone level above the page, and photograph each page without flash. You don’t need to take the prints out: a scanning app can then cut each print out of the page, straighten it, and save it as its own photo.

What you need

  • An iPhone
  • A table near a window
  • A soft, dry cloth for the plastic sleeves and for your camera lens
  • About a minute a page

Step by step

  1. Find even light. Daylight from a window works best, but not direct sun. Turn off the flash and any lamp right above you: both leave a bright spot on glossy prints and plastic sleeves.
  2. Lay the album flat. If it won’t open flat, prop the other side on a book so the page you’re scanning lies level.
  3. Wipe the sleeve and your lens. Dust and fingerprints show up in every photo.
  4. Hold the iPhone level, directly above the page, so the page’s edges look square on the screen. A tilted phone stretches the prints on the far side.
  5. Fill the screen with the page, and leave a little margin around it.
  6. If you see a bright spot of glare, move. Change your angle a little, shade the page with your body, or turn the album a quarter turn.
  7. Take the photo, check that it’s sharp, and turn the page.

Don’t plan to fix glare afterwards. A glare spot hides the picture under it, and no app can know what was there.

Should you take the photos out?

Usually not. In old “magnetic” albums with sticky pages, prints are often stuck fast, and pulling can tear them or peel the picture off its backing. Scan them where they are. If a print comes away easily and you want the best possible copy of it, scan it alone, out of the sleeve.

A page at a time, or a print at a time?

A whole page is much faster: a page with six prints takes one photo instead of six. Each print gets fewer of the camera’s pixels, which is fine for looking at on a phone, for sharing, and for reprints at the original size. For the few pictures you want to print large, scan them again one at a time, closer up.

Keep the order and the notes

Scan the pages in order, and include the captions and anything written beside the prints. If something is written on the back of a loose print, photograph the back too. Who is in a picture and when it was taken are worth as much as the picture.

How Our Decades does it

Questions

Can I scan photos without taking them out of the album?

Yes. Photograph the whole page through the sleeve, in even light with no flash, and move a little if you see glare. Prints in old sticky-page albums are safer left where they are.

Is an iPhone good enough, or do I need a flatbed scanner?

For looking, sharing and reprints at the original size, an iPhone is enough, and it’s many times faster. A flatbed scanner still records the most detail, so it’s worth using for the few prints you want to enlarge.

How do I stop glare when I photograph a glossy photo?

Turn off the flash, use daylight from a window, and hold the phone level above the print. If a bright spot still shows, change your angle or shade the print with your body until it’s gone.

How long does it take to scan a whole album?

Plan on about a minute a page once you have a rhythm. A 40-page album is one evening’s work.
