What AI photo restoration can and can’t do

By Our Decades · Updated November 25, 2026

AI restoration is good at damage: fading, color casts, scratches, dust and creases. It can’t recover what the camera never recorded, so a face that is blurred or missing in the print can only be guessed, and a guessed face is a different person.

What it does well

  • Fading and low contrast
  • Yellow, orange and red color casts
  • Scratches, dust, spots and creases
  • A little more sharpness in a soft or grainy print

Where it guesses

  • Blurred or very small faces. A tiny face in a group photo holds very little detail. A sharp face made from it is drawn, not recovered.
  • Missing parts. A torn-away corner can be filled in believably, but the fill is new.
  • Color for black-and-white photos. The colors are plausible, not known. Nobody recorded the color of that dress.
  • Fine detail. Lettering, patterns, jewelry and hands can come back slightly different.

The real risk: a stranger’s face

The common failure isn’t an ugly result. It’s a beautiful one in which your grandmother looks like someone else: smoother skin, sharper eyes, and a slightly different person. A family notices at once.

How to check any restored photo

  1. Put it next to the original, at the same size.
  2. Look at each face: the eyes, the mouth, the shape of the face, the glasses.
  3. Count things: people, fingers, buttons. Nothing should be added or gone.
  4. Look at the background for anything that wasn’t there.
  5. If a face looks better but less like the person, keep the original.
  6. Always keep the original scan, whatever you do to the copy.

Questions

Can AI restore a blurry face in an old photo?

It can make the face look sharp, but the detail is drawn, not recovered. If the print never held that detail, the sharp face is a guess, and it may not look like the person.

Does AI restoration change how people look?

It can. Check every face against the original at the same size, and keep the original scan.

Is AI colorization accurate?

The colors are plausible, not known. A black-and-white photo doesn’t record color, so any color added is a guess.

Is it safe to upload family photos to an AI restoration app?

It depends on the app. Ask where the photos go, whether they’re used to train AI, and whether you can delete them. Read the app’s privacy policy for the answers.
