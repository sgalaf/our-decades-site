Our Decades

Privacy

Your family's photos are yours. Here is everything we do with them, in plain words.

Our Decades is run by Redwood Oaks LLC ("we"), for people in the United States.

What we keep

  • The photos you scan or add, the restored copies we make, the backs of prints you scan, and the videos we make from them.
  • The stories you record or type. A recorded story's voice is kept so your family can play it; its words are written down on your iPhone (for a few languages, see AI services).
  • If you add photos from a WhatsApp chat, only its photos. The rest of the export, with the chat's messages, is deleted as soon as the photos are out.
  • The names, dates and places you add, your family's name, and the names you give the people in your photos.
  • Your name and email from Apple or Google when you sign in (Apple may give us a private relay email), and your profile photo from Google (Apple shares no photo). We show your name and photo only to you and your family in the app.
  • Your plan: Apple tells us what you bought and when it renews. We never see your card.
  • For your iPhone: a random id the app makes for it, its notification token if you allow notifications, and whether this iPhone already used its free first page (Apple's DeviceCheck keeps that, not the iPhone's identity).
  • What happens in the app, like "page scanned" or "plan started", to count how the app is used, and crash reports from iOS (where the app stopped, the iPhone model and iOS version; never a photo). Crash reports are kept 90 days.
  • If you leave your email on our-decades.com to hear when the app is out: that email, used once to tell you, then deleted.
  • When you tap "Get Our Decades" on an invite page, a scrambled form of your network's address for half an hour, so the app can offer that invite when you open it. Never the address itself.
  • If you try one photo on our website: that photo, restored on our server the same way as in the app (see AI services), and deleted for real within 24 hours, with its restored copies. Only your link shows it. We never train AI on it, and it's never kept for our tests. Choosing the photo agrees to its face check (our Terms): while it's restored, our server measures each face in it and compares the restored face with your print, so everyone still looks like themselves, as in the app (see Faces). Those measurements are deleted with the photo, within 24 hours. What stays: counts (a photo was tried, how long it took, whether you tapped Looks good or Not right), a random id your browser keeps so you can come back to it, and a scrambled form of your network's address, to limit how many photos one network tries in a day.

We don't collect your location. If a photo from your library says where it was taken, we remove that, and the rest of the file's details except which way is up, before we store it.

Faces

To find the same person across your family's photos and suggest their name, our server makes a "likeness" of each face in your photos: a list of numbers that describes the face. It can't be turned back into a picture.

  • We make likenesses only after you say yes: the app asks once, before you start ("Find everyone"). If you try a photo on our website, choosing it is that yes for that one photo (see above), and its likenesses are deleted with it within 24 hours.
  • They're made on our own server and kept in our database at Render, in the US (Ohio). They're shared with no one: used only to suggest names in your own family's photos, never sent to any other company, never sold, and never used to train any AI.
  • Photos that show faces still go to the AI services to be restored and brought to life. A likeness never does.
  • Saying no doesn't stop restoring: your photos still go to the AI services (the app asks about those separately). No likeness is made, and a restore fixes light and color but never touches faces. Bringing a photo to life, the moving pictures between photos in a video, and the first surprise animation need the face check, so they're skipped.
  • A likeness is deleted when its photo is deleted, when you delete that person, and when you delete your account or family. We don't keep it any longer than that.
  • To take back your yes: turn off Settings › Recognize faces, or write to privacy@our-decades.com. We then delete every likeness made from your photos.

While a photo is being restored, our server also checks each face in the new version against the original, so everyone still looks like themselves. The measurements of each face are thrown away when the restore ends. We keep only counts for each photo (how many faces were checked, how many passed, and why a try was turned down), which can't identify anyone.

Who sees it

  • Only the people in your family on Our Decades, and the people you send things to.
  • If you share photos as a link, anyone with that link can see those photos and their dates, nothing else, until you stop sharing it (Settings › Shared links).
  • An invite link shows your family's name and your first name, never your photos.
  • We look at your photos or stories only to fix a problem you tell us about, or when the law requires it.

AI services

Some of the work is done by AI services outside our own servers. The app asks you before the first photo goes to them, once, naming who gets what ("Agree and continue"). "Not now" sends nothing. Each gets only what its job needs:

  • fal.ai runs the AI models below for us. We send every request with fal's no-storage setting: photos go inside the request, never into fal's storage, fal keeps no history of the requests, and what it makes expires within the hour (we download it at once).
  • Restoring each new photo: the photo goes to FLUX.3 klein (Black Forest Labs) through fal.ai. If that model can't be used, we may use Seedream (ByteDance) instead, also through fal.ai. If you tap "Not right" and say what's wrong, your words go with it.
  • Bringing a photo to life, and videos: the photo (two neighbouring photos, for a video) goes to PixVerse through fal.ai. If PixVerse is unavailable, we may use Kling (Kuaishou) or MiniMax instead, also through fal.ai.
  • Writing down a story's voice: your iPhone does this itself in English and most languages. In a language it can't (Hebrew, for example), the voice goes to Whisper (OpenAI's open model, run by fal.ai), through a link to our storage that stops working after 10 minutes.
  • Reading a story and a print's back: Claude, from Anthropic, reads a story's words (typed, or written down from your voice; never the voice itself or the photo) to suggest names, dates and places. If your iPhone can't read the writing on a print's back, a picture of the back goes to Claude to read it.

We never use your photos, voice or words to train AI. Under Anthropic's terms for business customers, Anthropic doesn't train its AI on what we send.

Companies that hold data for us

  • Render runs our servers and database (in the US, Ohio).
  • Cloudflare R2 stores the files: photos, voices and videos.
  • Apple: sign-in, purchases, notifications, and DeviceCheck. Google: sign-in.

We have no ads and no ad or analytics companies in the app, and we never sell or share your data for ads.

Making Our Decades better

We never train AI on your photos: no model of ours learns from them. We do use them, on our own server, with what you tell us about the results ("Looks good", "Not right"), to improve how we restore: the instructions we give the models, the checks a restore must pass, and the tests we grade them with. That stops when the photo is deleted.

Deleting

  • Deleting a photo deletes it, its copies, its videos and its stories' voices.
  • Settings › Delete my account and photos deletes your photos, stories, videos, your profile photo, the links you shared and the families you made (for everyone in them), and the photos you added to other families. It happens on our servers right away, and the files are erased, not hidden. Deleting doesn't cancel a plan: cancel it with Apple too.
  • What's left after that: counts of what happened in the app, with nothing that links them to you.

Children

Our Decades is for adults. It isn't meant for children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect information from them. Family photos can show children; they're seen only by your family and the people you share with.

Your rights

You can see, change and delete everything in the app. For anything else, or to ask what we hold about you, write to us.
